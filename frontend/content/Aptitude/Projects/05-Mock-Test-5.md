## Section A: Quantitative Aptitude (Q1-15)

1. Find the unit digit of 42⁶⁶.

2. The HCF of two numbers is 15 and their LCM is 180. If one of the numbers is 45, find the other number.
   A) 50  B) 60  C) 65  D) 75

3. Simplify: 25 − 10 ÷ 5 × 3 + 7
   A) 24  B) 26  C) 28  D) 30

4. Find the value of 45% of 180 + 35% of 200.

5. A shopkeeper marks an item 30% above its cost price, then offers a 20% discount on the marked price. Find his overall profit percent.
   A) 4%  B) 6%  C) 8%  D) 10%

6. In what ratio must sugar worth ₹50/kg be mixed with sugar worth ₹65/kg so that the mixture is worth ₹58/kg?
   A) 7:8  B) 8:7  C) 1:1  D) 5:3

7. If A:B = 3:4 and B:C = 5:6, find A:B:C.
   A) 15:20:24  B) 3:4:6  C) 15:24:20  D) 3:20:24

8. A invests ₹9000 for 7 months and B invests ₹7000 for 9 months in a business. Find the ratio of their profit shares.
   A) 1:1  B) 7:9  C) 9:7  D) 63:70

9. A car travels at 65 km/h for 3 hours. Find the distance covered.
   A) 185 km  B) 190 km  C) 195 km  D) 200 km

10. A train 240 m long crosses a pole in 8 seconds. Find its speed in km/h.
    A) 96 km/h  B) 102 km/h  C) 108 km/h  D) 114 km/h

11. A can complete a piece of work in 14 days and B can complete it in 35 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it?
    A) 8 days  B) 9 days  C) 10 days  D) 12 days

12. Find the compound interest on ₹2500 for 2 years at 8% per annum, compounded annually.
    A) ₹400  B) ₹408  C) ₹416  D) ₹424

13. A container has 60 liters of pure milk. 30 liters are removed and replaced with water; this operation is repeated once more (a total of two times). How much pure milk remains?

14. A right-angled triangle has legs of 9 cm and 40 cm. Find its hypotenuse.

## Answer Key

1. **B) 4** — The unit digit of powers of 2 cycles as 2, 4, 8, 6 (period 4). 66 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 2, so the answer is the 2nd term of the cycle: 4.
2. **B) 60** — HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers, so the other number = (15 × 180) ÷ 45 = 60. (Check: HCF(45, 60) = 15, LCM(45, 60) = 180. ✓)
3. **B) 26** — Division and multiplication left to right first: 10 ÷ 5 × 3 = 6; then 25 − 6 + 7 = 26.
4. **C) 151** — 45% of 180 = 81; 35% of 200 = 70; total = 151.
5. **A) 4%** — CP = 100; MP = 130 (30% above CP); SP = 130 × 0.8 = 104; profit = 4, so profit% = 4%.
6. **A) 7:8** — Alligation: Cheaper : Dearer = (65−58) : (58−50) = 7 : 8.
7. **A) 15:20:24** — A:B = 3:4 = 15:20; B:C = 5:6 = 20:24; combining on the common B value (20) gives A:B:C = 15:20:24.
8. **A) 1:1** — Profit ratio = (investment × time): A = 9000×7 = 63000; B = 7000×9 = 63000; ratio = 63000:63000 = 1:1.
9. **C) 195 km** — Distance = speed × time = 65 × 3 = 195 km.
10. **C) 108 km/h** — Speed = 240 m ÷ 8 s = 30 m/s; converting, 30 × 18/5 = 108 km/h.
11. **C) 10 days** — Combined rate = 1/14 + 1/35 = 5/70 + 2/70 = 7/70 = 1/10, so together they take 10 days.
12. **C) ₹416** — CI = P(1+R/100)ᵀ − P = 2500×(1.08)² − 2500 = 2916 − 2500 = 416.
13. **B) 15 L** — Each removal-and-replace step multiplies remaining milk by (1 − 30/60) = 0.5. After 2 steps: 60 × 0.5² = 60 × 0.25 = 15 L.
14. **C) 41 cm** — 9, 40, 41 is a standard Pythagorean triple: 9² + 40² = 81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41².
15. **A) 14/33** — P(both red) = C(8,2)/C(12,2) = 28/66 = 14/33.
16. **C) 47** — Each term is the sum of the previous two terms: 4+7=11, 7+11=18, 11+18=29, 18+29=47.
17. **C) M** — Alphabet positions Y(25), V(22), S(19), P(16) each drop by a fixed 3; the next term is 16−3=13 = M.
18. **A) HQTHP** — Confirm the +5 shift from TRAIN→YWFNS, then apply it to CLOCK: C→H, L→Q, O→T, C→H, K→P.
19. **A) Sister** — P and Q are siblings (P is brother of Q), so R (father of P) is also the father of Q. S, the daughter of R, is therefore also Q's sibling — and since S is female, S is Q's sister.
20. **B) 17 km** — The two legs (8 km and 15 km) are at right angles, so the straight-line distance is √(8²+15²) = √289 = 17 km (the 8-15-17 triple).
21. **A) V, W, O, N, X, M** — V at the left end gives seat 1; W immediately right of V gives seat 2; exactly two people to the left of O means O is seat 3; N immediately right of O gives seat 4; M at the right end gives seat 6; the only remaining seat (5) goes to X.
22. **D) Neither I nor II follows** — The middle term "pencils" is undistributed in both statements (predicate of the first, subject of the second), so a valid diagram exists where the eraser-overlap with pencils sits entirely outside the pen-subset of pencils (breaking I), and "All pencils are pens" (II) is simply the unsupported converse of statement 1, false in any diagram where pencils has non-pen members (which statement 1 allows).
23. **C) Both I and II follow** — Both statements are universal, so only one diagram is possible: tigers sit entirely inside mammals, and mammals are entirely separate from plants, so tigers are automatically separate from plants, giving "No tiger is a plant" (I). Since tigers exist (by the assertion of statement 1) and none of them are plants, at least one mammal (a tiger) is not a plant, giving "Some mammals are not plants" (II) as well.
24. **A) Statement I alone is sufficient, but II alone is not** — Statement I alone: m + 5 = 12 gives the unique value m = 7 — sufficient. Statement II alone: "m is a positive integer" allows infinitely many values (1, 2, 3, ...) — not sufficient.
25. **D) Left edge** — Tracking the star's position only: top → right → bottom is a 90° clockwise move each step. One more 90° clockwise move from bottom lands on the left edge.
26. **C) Part C** — The sentence lists three things the team was asked to do and should be parallel in form ("to finish... review... and submit"), but Part C breaks the pattern with "reviewing the data" instead of "review the data."
27. **C) The results of the match surprised everyone.** — This is the only option free of redundancy: (A) "final outcome" is redundant since an outcome is already the final result, (B) "advance planning" is redundant since planning is inherently forward-looking, (D) "close proximity" is redundant since proximity already means closeness.
28. **B) Part B** — Covering the modifier "After finishing the presentation" and reading "the audience's questions caught the speaker completely off guard" shows the audience's questions cannot be the thing that finished the presentation — a dangling modifier; the noun following the phrase should refer to the speaker, not "the audience's questions."
29. **B) Spiteful** — "Mal-" (Latin, "bad") signals a negative quality directed at others; "malicious" means intending harm, i.e., spiteful. (A) and (C) are the opposite direction, and (D) is unrelated to the root's meaning.
30. **B) Reveal a secret** — "Spill the beans" is a fixed idiom; a literal reading (spilling beans) is nonsensical here, and the context ("promised not to... about the surprise party") confirms the idiom means revealing a secret.
