## Section A: Quantitative Aptitude (Q1-15)

1. Find the unit digit of 13⁸⁹.
   A) 1  B) 3  C) 7  D) 9

2. The HCF of two numbers is 6 and their LCM is 252. If one of the numbers is 42, find the other number.
   A) 30  B) 32  C) 36  D) 40

3. Simplify: 20 − 8 ÷ 2 × 3 + 6
   A) 10  B) 12  C) 14  D) 16

4. Find the value of 20% of 350 + 60% of 150.
   A) 140  B) 150  C) 160  D) 170

5. A shopkeeper marks an item 50% above its cost price, then offers a 10% discount on the marked price. Find his overall profit percent.
   A) 25%  B) 30%  C) 35%  D) 40%

6. In what ratio must tea worth ₹45/kg be mixed with tea worth ₹70/kg so that the mixture is worth ₹55/kg?
   A) 3:2  B) 2:3  C) 1:1  D) 5:3

7. If A:B = 5:6 and B:C = 4:7, find A:B:C.
   A) 10:12:21  B) 10:12:14  C) 5:6:7  D) 10:6:21

8. A invests ₹4000 for 6 months and B invests ₹5000 for 9 months in a business. Find the ratio of their profit shares.
   A) 8:15  B) 15:8  C) 4:5  D) 6:5

9. A car travels at 72 km/h for 4.5 hours. Find the distance covered.
   A) 300 km  B) 312 km  C) 324 km  D) 336 km

10. A train 250 m long crosses a pole in 10 seconds. Find its speed in km/h.
    A) 75 km/h  B) 80 km/h  C) 85 km/h  D) 90 km/h

11. A can complete a piece of work in 20 days and B can complete it in 30 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it?
    A) 10 days  B) 12 days  C) 15 days  D) 18 days

12. Find the compound interest on ₹1500 for 2 years at 10% per annum, compounded annually.
    A) ₹300  B) ₹310  C) ₹315  D) ₹320

13. A container has 90 liters of pure milk. 30 liters are removed and replaced with water; this operation is repeated once more (a total of two times). How much pure milk remains?
    A) 36 L  B) 40 L  C) 45 L  D) 50 L

14. A right-angled triangle has legs of 20 cm and 21 cm. Find its hypotenuse.
    A) 27 cm  B) 28 cm  C) 29 cm  D) 30 cm

15. A bag contains 4 red and 6 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. Find the probability that both are blue.
    A) 1/4  B) 3/10  C) 1/3  D) 2/5

## Answer Key

1. **B) 3** — Unit digit of powers of 13 follows the same cycle as powers of 3: 3, 9, 7, 1 (period 4). 89 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 1, so the answer is the 1st term of the cycle: 3.
2. **C) 36** — HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers, so the other number = (6 × 252) ÷ 42 = 36. (Check: HCF(42, 36) = 6, LCM(42, 36) = 252. ✓)
3. **C) 14** — Division and multiplication left to right first: 8 ÷ 2 × 3 = 12; then 20 − 12 + 6 = 14.
4. **C) 160** — 20% of 350 = 70; 60% of 150 = 90; total = 160.
5. **C) 35%** — CP = 100; MP = 150 (50% above CP); SP = 150 × 0.9 = 135; profit = 35, so profit% = 35%.
6. **A) 3:2** — Alligation: Cheaper : Dearer = (70−55) : (55−45) = 15 : 10 = 3:2.
7. **A) 10:12:21** — A:B = 5:6 = 10:12; B:C = 4:7 = 12:21; combining on the common B value (12) gives A:B:C = 10:12:21.
8. **A) 8:15** — Profit ratio = (investment × time): A = 4000×6 = 24000; B = 5000×9 = 45000; ratio = 24000:45000 = 8:15.
9. **C) 324 km** — Distance = speed × time = 72 × 4.5 = 324 km.
10. **D) 90 km/h** — Speed = 250 m ÷ 10 s = 25 m/s; converting, 25 × 18/5 = 90 km/h.
11. **B) 12 days** — Combined rate = 1/20 + 1/30 = 3/60 + 2/60 = 5/60 = 1/12, so together they take 12 days.
12. **C) ₹315** — CI = P(1+R/100)ᵀ − P = 1500×(1.1)² − 1500 = 1815 − 1500 = 315.
13. **B) 40 L** — Each removal-and-replace step multiplies remaining milk by (1 − 30/90) = 2/3. After 2 steps: 90 × (2/3)² = 90 × 4/9 = 40 L.
14. **C) 29 cm** — 20, 21, 29 is a standard Pythagorean triple: 20² + 21² = 400 + 441 = 841 = 29².
15. **C) 1/3** — P(both blue) = C(6,2)/C(10,2) = 15/45 = 1/3.
16. **C) 95** — Each term is double the previous term plus 1: 5×2+1=11, 11×2+1=23, 23×2+1=47, 47×2+1=95.
17. **B) P** — Alphabet positions Z(26), Y(25), W(23), T(20) drop by 1, 2, 3 respectively; the drop itself increases by 1 each step, so the next drop is 4, giving position 20−4=16 = P.
18. **A) PRQHB** — Confirm the +3 shift from WATER→ZDWHU (W→Z, A→D, T→W, E→H, R→U), then apply it to MONEY: M→P, O→R, N→Q, E→H, Y→B (wrapping past Z).
19. **A) Grandfather** — "R × Q" (R is mother of Q) fixes R as female, and "P + R" (P is father of R) makes R the daughter of P; so P is the father of Q's mother, i.e., P is Q's grandfather.
20. **C) 10 km** — The two legs (6 km and 8 km) are at right angles (a 90° turn), so the straight-line distance is √(6²+8²) = √100 = 10 km (the 6-8-10 triple).
21. **A) J, K, L, N, M** — K is second from the left (seat 2); L immediately right of K gives seat 3; M at the right end gives seat 5; N immediately left of M gives seat 4; the only remaining seat (1) goes to J, giving the order J, K, L, N, M.
22. **D) Neither I nor II follows** — The middle term "plates" is undistributed in both statements (predicate of the A-statement, subject of the I-statement), so a valid diagram exists where the spoon-overlap with plates sits entirely outside the cup-subset of plates (breaking I), and "All plates are cups" (II) is simply the unsupported converse of statement 1, false in any diagram where plates has non-cup members (which statement 1 allows).
23. **C) Both I and II follow** — Both statements are universal, so only one diagram is possible: doctors sit entirely inside engineers, and engineers are entirely separate from farmers, so doctors are automatically separate from farmers, giving "No doctor is a farmer" (I). Since doctors exist (by the assertion of statement 1) and none of them are farmers, at least one engineer (a doctor) is not a farmer, giving "Some engineers are not farmers" (II) as well.
24. **E) Statements I and II together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient** — Statement I alone: x² = 49 gives x = 7 or x = −7, two values — not sufficient. Statement II alone (forgetting Statement I): "positive and less than 10" allows many values (1, 2, 3, ... and non-integers) — not sufficient. Combined: x² = 49 restricts x to ±7, and "positive" eliminates −7, leaving the unique value x = 7 — sufficient only when combined.
25. **A) North (↑)** — Tracking the arrow's direction only: East → South → West is a 90° clockwise turn each step. One more 90° clockwise turn from West (←) lands on North (↑).
26. **C) Necessary** — Correct spelling has one "c" and two "s"s: nec-e-ss-ary.
27. **B) does** — Third-person singular subject "He" takes the verb form "does" in the present simple tense.
28. **A) Fake** — "Genuine" means real or authentic; its opposite is "Fake." ("Real," "True," and "Authentic" are synonyms of "Genuine," not antonyms.)
29. **C) They don't play football.** — Plural subject "They" takes the auxiliary "don't," followed by the base form of the verb "play."
30. **B) Courageous** — "Brave" and "Courageous" both describe fearlessness in the face of danger; the other options describe the opposite trait.
