## 1. Concept

Partnership questions are ratio-division questions in disguise: two or more people invest money in a business and the profit (or loss) has to be split fairly. The "fair" split isn't equal — it's proportional to how much *and how long* each person's money was actually at work in the business. This topic is really just an application of everything from the ratio lesson, layered onto a business story.

There are two flavors. In **simple partnership**, everyone invests for the same length of time (say, the whole year), so the profit is split purely by the capital ratio. In **compound partnership**, people invest different amounts for different lengths of time (someone joins later, or withdraws early, or a partner also draws a fixed salary for running the business) — here you must weight each person's capital by the time it was invested before taking the ratio.

**Plain-English example:** Two friends open a shop. One puts in ₹1,00,000 and works the full year; the other puts in ₹1,00,000 too but joins six months late. Even though they invested the same *amount*, it would be unfair to split profit equally — the first friend's money (and often, effort) was at risk for twice as long. That's exactly what capital × time weighting corrects for.

## 2. Shortcut / Trick

### Simple partnership (equal time)

When all partners invest for the same duration, profit is split directly in the ratio of their capitals:

$$\text{Share of A} = \text{Total profit} \times \frac{\text{A's capital}}{\text{Sum of all capitals}}$$

### Compound partnership (capital × time)

When investment periods differ, first convert each partner's investment into "capital-months" (or capital-years) by multiplying capital by the time it was invested, **then** take the ratio of these products:

$$\text{Ratio of profit shares} = C_A \times T_A \;:\; C_B \times T_B \;:\; C_C \times T_C$$

A partner who joins later or withdraws early simply gets a smaller time value plugged in — don't adjust the capital, adjust the time.

### Working-partner adjustment (fixed fee before split)

If one partner is also paid a fixed salary/commission for managing the business, that amount is paid out **first**, off the top of the total profit. Only the *remaining* profit is then split by the capital×time ratio:

$$\text{Remaining profit} = \text{Total profit} - \text{Fixed fee}$$

then apply the normal capital(×time) ratio to that remaining amount, and finally add the fixed fee back on top of the working partner's share.

## 3. Worked Examples

### (a) Simple partnership — equal time, different capitals

A invests ₹5,000 and B invests ₹7,000 in a business for one full year. If the year's profit is ₹6,000, find each partner's share.

Since both invested for the same time, split by capital ratio: 5,000 : 7,000 = 5 : 7. Sum of parts = 12.

- A's share = 6000 × 5/12 = **₹2,500**
- B's share = 6000 × 7/12 = **₹3,500**

**Check:** 2500 + 3500 = 6000. Matches.

### (b) Compound partnership — one partner joins later

A invests ₹20,000 for the full year (12 months). B invests ₹15,000 but joins the business after 3 months, so his money is invested for only 9 months. If the year's profit is ₹25,000, find each partner's share.

Convert to capital-months:

- A: 20,000 × 12 = 2,40,000
- B: 15,000 × 9 = 1,35,000

Ratio = 2,40,000 : 1,35,000. Divide both by 15,000 → **16 : 9**. Sum of parts = 25.

- A's share = 25,000 × 16/25 = **₹16,000**
- B's share = 25,000 × 9/25 = **₹9,000**

**Check:** 16,000 + 9,000 = 25,000. Matches — and note A's share (16/25 = 64%) is more than double B's, even though A's capital (20,000) was less than 1.5× B's (15,000) — the extra 3 months at work made the real difference.

### (c) Working partner also gets a fixed management fee

A and B invest ₹40,000 and ₹60,000 respectively for the full year in a business. A also manages daily operations and is paid a fixed salary of ₹12,000 for the year before any profit split. If the year's total profit (before the salary is paid) is ₹50,000, find each partner's final share.

Step 1 — pay the fixed fee first: Remaining profit = 50,000 − 12,000 = 38,000.

Step 2 — split the remainder by capital ratio (equal time, so just capitals): 40,000 : 60,000 = 2 : 3. Sum of parts = 5.

- A's share of remainder = 38,000 × 2/5 = 15,200
- B's share of remainder = 38,000 × 3/5 = 22,800

Step 3 — add A's fixed salary back on top of A's share:

- A's total = 12,000 + 15,200 = **₹27,200**
- B's total = **₹22,800**

**Check:** 27,200 + 22,800 = 50,000. Matches — the full profit is accounted for.

## 4. Timed Practice Set

1. A and B invest ₹8,000 and ₹12,000 respectively for one year. If the profit is ₹15,000, find each partner's share. (45 sec)
2. A, B, and C invest ₹10,000, ₹15,000, and ₹25,000 respectively for one year. Find each partner's share of a ₹20,000 profit. (60 sec)
3. A invests ₹40,000 for the full year; B invests ₹30,000 but joins after 4 months (so is invested for 8 months). Find the profit-sharing ratio. (60 sec)
4. Using the ratio from question 3, if the year's profit is ₹36,000, find each partner's share. (45 sec)
5. A invests ₹50,000 for 12 months and B invests ₹40,000 for 6 months. If the profit is ₹21,000, find each partner's share. (60 sec)
6. A and B are partners with capitals of ₹60,000 and ₹90,000 invested for the full year. A also draws a fixed ₹5,000 as management fee before any split. If total profit is ₹35,000, find each partner's final share. (75 sec)

### Answer Key

1. **A: ₹6,000, B: ₹9,000** — capital ratio 8000:12000 = 2:3, sum 5; 15000×2/5=6000, 15000×3/5=9000.
2. **A: ₹4,000, B: ₹6,000, C: ₹10,000** — capital ratio 10000:15000:25000 = 2:3:5, sum 10; 20000×2/10=4000, ×3/10=6000, ×5/10=10000.
3. **2 : 1** — capital-months: A = 40000×12 = 480000; B = 30000×8 = 240000 (invested 12−4=8 months); ratio 480000:240000 = 2:1.
4. **A: ₹24,000, B: ₹12,000** — using the 2:1 ratio from Q3, sum of parts = 3; 36000×2/3=24000, 36000×1/3=12000.
5. **A: ₹15,000, B: ₹6,000** — capital-months: A=50000×12=600000, B=40000×6=240000; ratio 600000:240000=5:2, sum 7; 21000×5/7=15000, 21000×2/7=6000.
6. **A: ₹17,000, B: ₹18,000** — remaining profit = 35000−5000=30000; capital ratio 60000:90000=2:3, sum 5; A=30000×2/5=12000+5000 fee=17000; B=30000×3/5=18000.
