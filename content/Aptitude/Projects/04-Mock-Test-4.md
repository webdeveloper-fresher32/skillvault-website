## Answer Key

1. **B) 4** — The unit digit of powers of 4 cycles as 4, 6 (period 2). 35 is odd, so the answer is the 1st term of the cycle: 4.
2. **C) 54** — HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers, so the other number = (9 × 270) ÷ 45 = 54. (Check: HCF(45, 54) = 9, LCM(45, 54) = 270. ✓)
3. **B) 21** — Division and multiplication left to right first: 12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 8; then 18 + 8 − 5 = 21.
4. **B) 115** — 35% of 200 = 70; 15% of 300 = 45; total = 115.
5. **C) 19%** — CP = 100; MP = 140 (40% above CP); SP = 140 × 0.85 = 119; profit = 19, so profit% = 19%.
6. **B) 2:3** — Alligation: Cheaper : Dearer = (100−92) : (92−80) = 8 : 12 = 2:3.
7. **A) 10:15:21** — A:B = 2:3 = 10:15; B:C = 5:7 = 15:21; combining on the common B value (15) gives A:B:C = 10:15:21.
8. **A) 4:3** — Profit ratio = (investment × time): A = 8000×9 = 72000; B = 9000×6 = 54000; ratio = 72000:54000 = 4:3.
9. **C) 270 km** — Distance = speed × time = 54 × 5 = 270 km.
10. **C) 72 km/h** — Speed = 220 m ÷ 11 s = 20 m/s; converting, 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h.
11. **B) 12 days** — Combined rate = 1/21 + 1/28 = 4/84 + 3/84 = 7/84 = 1/12, so together they take 12 days.
12. **C) ₹410** — CI = P(1+R/100)ᵀ − P = 4000×(1.05)² − 4000 = 4410 − 4000 = 410.
13. **B) 45 L** — Each removal-and-replace step multiplies remaining milk by (1 − 20/80) = 0.75. After 2 steps: 80 × 0.75² = 80 × 0.5625 = 45 L.
14. **C) 25 cm** — 7, 24, 25 is a standard Pythagorean triple: 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25².
15. **B) 7/22** — P(both red) = C(7,2)/C(12,2) = 21/66 = 7/22.
16. **C) 63** — Each term is the sum of the previous two terms: 6+9=15, 9+15=24, 15+24=39, 24+39=63.
17. **C) V** — Alphabet positions D(4), G(7), K(11), P(16) have differences 3, 4, 5; the differences themselves increase by 1, so the next difference is 6, giving position 16+6=22 = V.
18. **A) QMRH** — Confirm the +4 shift from BOOK→FSSO, then apply it to MIND: M→Q, I→M, N→R, D→H.
19. **A) Mother** — "The only daughter of my mother" must be the woman herself, since her mother has only one daughter. So the man's mother is the woman herself, meaning the woman is the man's mother.
20. **B) 13 km** — Facing North and turning right means now facing East; the two legs (5 km and 12 km) are at right angles, so the straight-line distance is √(5²+12²) = √169 = 13 km (the 5-12-13 triple).
21. **A) G, H, J, K, I, L** — Exactly three people to the right of J means J is seat 3 (of 6); K immediately right of J gives seat 4; G at the left end gives seat 1; L at the right end gives seat 6; I immediately left of L gives seat 5; the only remaining seat (2) goes to H.
22. **D) Neither I nor II follows** — The middle term "journals" is undistributed in both statements (predicate of the first, subject of the second), so a valid diagram exists where the magazine-overlap with journals sits entirely outside the book-subset of journals (breaking I), and "All journals are books" (II) is simply the unsupported converse of statement 1, false in any diagram where journals has non-book members (which statement 1 allows).
23. **C) Both I and II follow** — Both statements are universal, so only one diagram is possible: laptops sit entirely inside gadgets, and gadgets are entirely separate from furniture, so laptops are automatically separate from furniture, giving "No laptop is furniture" (I). Since laptops exist (by the assertion of statement 1) and none of them are furniture, at least one gadget (a laptop) is not furniture, giving "Some gadgets are not furniture" (II) as well.
24. **E) Statements I and II together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient** — Statement I alone: y² = 64 gives y = 8 or y = −8, two values — not sufficient. Statement II alone: "negative and greater than −10" allows many values (−1, −2, ..., −9, and non-integers) — not sufficient. Combined: y² = 64 restricts y to ±8, and "negative" eliminates 8, leaving the unique value y = −8 — sufficient only when combined.
25. **D) Bottom-left** — Tracking the dot's position only: top-left → top-right → bottom-right is a 90° clockwise move each step. One more 90° clockwise move from bottom-right lands on bottom-left.
26. **C) Part C** — "The population of Mumbai" (a population) is being compared directly to "Delhi" (a city) instead of to "the population of Delhi" — insert "that of": "than **that of** Delhi."
27. **C) All students must submit the form by Friday.** — This is the only option free of redundancy: (A) "each and every" repeats the same meaning twice, (B) "past history" is redundant since history is already about the past, (D) "repeat again" is redundant since "repeat" already means "say again."
28. **B) Part B** — Covering the modifier "Arriving late for the meeting" and reading "the manager's laptop was already displaying the opening presentation slide" shows a laptop cannot be the one arriving late — a dangling modifier; the noun following the phrase should be "the manager," not "the manager's laptop."
29. **B) Kind-hearted** — "Bene-" (Latin, "good/well") signals a positive quality toward others; "benevolent" means wishing others well, i.e., kind-hearted. (A) is the opposite direction, and (C) and (D) are unrelated to the root's meaning.
30. **B) Avoid saying something directly; delay getting to the point** — "Beat around the bush" is a fixed idiom; a literal reading (walking around a shrub) is nonsensical here, and the context ("tell me directly instead of...") confirms the idiom means avoiding directness.
